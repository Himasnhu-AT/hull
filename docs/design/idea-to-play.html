Play it, then say what is off

Today the Studio is an editor: six benches, an Inspector with every field, a console. This turns it around. The game is the screen, you say what feels wrong in your own words, and everything the builder made is laid out to look at and listen to. The mock below is live: the game runs, the sounds play, and three example notes change it.

      Where today's Studio goes

      Up front

      • The gameWas one of six view tabs. Now it is the screen.
      • BuilderWas a floating panel, closed by default, with an agent picker. Now a fixed column that is already running.
      • EverythingReplaces the Library, the Content Browser and the Sound, Character, VFX and Look benches. The benches become the previews on the cards.
      • ChangesReplaces the Changes and History tabs with one list in plain words. Put back is the existing history revert.
      • ShareThe Build button, renamed for what it gives you.

      Behind an Advanced switch

      • Inspector with every fieldCards keep one or two sliders under Fine tune. The full form stays for whoever wants it.
      • Console, files and code viewThe builder reads problems itself. You see them only if it gets stuck.
      • Step one tick, quarter speed, launch optionsTools for the builder and for debugging, not for playing.
      • Experiments and CompareUseful, but a second idea to learn. Keep and Put back cover the common case.

      New to build

      • Just now feedThe running game reports the sounds, animations, effects and actors of the last few seconds over the dev protocol.
      • Freeze and noteOne key the engine reserves. It pauses the game and attaches a screenshot, the game time and the Just now list to your note.
      • A card preview per kindSound, character, effect and look reuse their benches. Animation needs a small looping player. Places need a camera shot.
      • Show in gameEach piece needs a moment that shows it: a start point, or a demo scene from its kit.
      • Cards inside the chatThe builder sends questions, the brief and approvals as small pieces of interface, in the style of AG-UI events, and its text streams as it is written.
      • Explained piecesFor rules, behaviour and places the builder writes a flowchart or a map with a plain explanation, kept with the project.
      • Builder always onStart the agent with the project and store the key in the app, so there is nothing to pick.
